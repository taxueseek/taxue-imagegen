# 手绘百相图 · 完整提示词 V2(高密度 80–100 · 四修复版)

> 相比 v1 的四处强化：① 面部五模板共存 ② 四段年龄配额 ③ 非坐姿至少 1/4 ④ 点色每色 8–10 次配额
> 可直接整段提交给图像模型。

```
An extremely dense, full-bleed, hand-drawn crowd portrait poster, vertical 2:3, on a pure white
ground (kept white, not cream, not ivory, no warm tint bleeding off the figures): roughly eighty
to a hundred clearly readable characters — humans of every age and body type, true animals
(birds, cats, dogs) and a smaller number of humanoid characters — packed together as one
interlocking "hundred faces" community picture, not a scene with a background, not a lineup, not
a grid.

All characters share the exact same hand-drawn language: loose, slightly trembling deep grey-black
ink contour lines with subtle weight variation and gentle asymmetry, filled with flat matte
opaque watercolor or marker color, features reduced to the essential, details kept restrained.
Every human, animal and humanoid sits at the same low level of simplification; the charm —
blank, curious, sleepy, deadpan or sly — is carried by posture and the tilt of the head rather
than by facial detail. No anime fine rendering, no pretty idealized faces, no realistic lighting
or shading, no 3D, no smooth vector curves, no airbrush, no heavy texture, no complex patterned
costumes.

Composition: the sheet is packed edge to edge with mostly small and medium figures, with a
handful of larger ones interleaved for rhythm; bodies interlock like differently shaped pieces of
a hand-drawn jigsaw puzzle, each yielding space to its neighbours — round-plump next to long-thin,
lying next to standing, spread-open next to curled-up, front view next to profile next to back
view. The figures fill the sheet so completely that the white ground survives only as thin
winding seams and small irregular pockets running between the bodies, never as a wide outer halo;
the outer margin stays narrow and organic, with only a few characters grazing the edges, never
cleanly cut off. No grid, no rows, no parade line, no shared baseline, no equidistant spacing, no
single central giant protagonist, no large empty blocks. Light overlapping is allowed when it
helps the interlock, but every face and every identifying feature stays visible; limbs never
merge into one another and bodies are never wrongly connected.

Four age tiers must all be clearly present and intermingled, roughly a quarter of the sheet each:
(1) clearly elderly people — stooped backs, bent knees, wrinkles hinted by a few lines, grey or
white hair, some leaning on walking sticks; (2) clearly full-grown adults, one in four to one in
three of them conventionally handsome adult men and conventionally attractive adult women whose
different jawlines, noses, hairstyles and builds make each read as a distinct adult person, never
one pretty template repeated; (3) small round children with biggish heads and stubby limbs; (4)
lanky teenagers with long limbs and a few with pigtails or caps of hair. Humans vary by body
shape (round, wiry, tall, stooped, pot-bellied), by face shape, hairstyle and temperament. Most
human figures do NOT simply sit cross-legged: at least a quarter of the whole sheet is caught in
non-seated poses — lying flat on the belly or side, stretched with arms up, bending forward from
the waist, leaning sideways, sprawled, mid-stride, squatting low, or curled up small — so the
sheet mixes seated, lying, standing and moving bodies instead of one uniform pose.

Faces are never one copied template: at least five clearly different face templates coexist and
recur in moderation across the sheet — round faces with two dot eyes and a tiny curved mouth; long
narrow faces with small horizontal-dash eyes and a straight mouth; wide faces with two small
round eyes set far apart and a small open mouth; heart-shaped faces with one-curve eyes and a
wavy mouth; and a few nearly blank faces with almost no features at all. Eye shapes vary between
round dots, horizontal dashes, closed slits and wide circles; mouth shapes vary between straight
lines, small curves, open circles, tiny triangles and no mouth at all; face proportions vary
between long oval, round, square, heart and tiny; some faces have a small nose dot, some none. No
two neighbouring characters share the same silhouette, pose, coloring or face template, and never
a face simply recolored.

Animals keep their true species structure — a cat curled into a closed loop, a dachshund
stretched long and low on tiny legs, a bird perched or preening with folded wings, a heavy hound
with drooping ears, a hovering hummingbird, a puppy mid-pounce, a cat stretching upward against
nothing — never standing on two legs, never wearing clothes. Humanoid characters each follow
their own independent logic of form — a figure built from branch and leaf, a round blunt-shelled
robot with a single lens eye, a long-necked figure with an animal head on a human body — each
with its own head-to-body proportion, never a human with a few swapped features. The humans,
humanoids and animals are distributed across the whole sheet in small interacting clusters — two
children pointing at a bird, an elderly person sharing a seat with a cat, a small robot playing
with a dog — so relationships grow out of these little groups instead of one hero surrounded by
an anonymous crowd.

Color stays natural and quiet: a shared low-to-mid-saturation base of cream white, grey-brown,
ochre brown, misty blue, blue-grey and soft green, applied as real skin, fur, feather and fabric
tones. Skin tones vary from pale cream to tan to ochre across the crowd rather than one flat
flesh color. The four accent colors — mustard yellow, coral orange, brick red and a clear bright
blue — must each recur at least eight to ten times across the sheet as small but clearly readable
color notes placed in real clothing, fur, feather, hats and accessories, spread out over the whole
picture rather than pooled in one region or left as one or two lonely speckles. Darker greys and
near-blacks are scattered as separate anchoring notes across the sheet rather than pooled into
one dark mass, and light figures are spaced through the picture to let it breathe. The sheet must
NOT read as one uniform brown-grey wash: warm and cool, dark and light clearly balance each other
across the whole picture, never grey and washed out, never blazing with saturated color.

Rather than cutting characters for tidiness, keep every single one readable: when the sheet gets
too busy, reduce each figure's internal detail first. Seen from a distance the sheet reads as one
calm, harmonious whole; up close every single character is different from every other — dense
without blurring, full without choking, the breathing space living in the seams.

No text, no letters, no labels, no captions, no numbers, no watermark, no signature, no frame,
no border, no sticker outline, no die-cut white edge, no drop shadow, no glow, no scenery, no
ground, no sky, no background environment, no flowers, no stars, no hearts, no decorative filler
of any kind, no heavy props, no garments beyond the simplest plain clothing.
```
