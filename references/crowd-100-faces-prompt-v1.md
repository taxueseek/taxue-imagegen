# 手绘百相图 · 可直接出图的完整提示词（竖版 2:3 · 高密度 80–100）

> 主题：鸟 / 猫 / 狗 / 成年俊男靓女 / 男女老少 / 人类＋类人＋动物主角团
> 英文成文（类型 B 硬规则）。正向与负面约束已自然融为一体，可直接整段提交给图像模型。

```
An extremely dense, full-bleed, hand-drawn crowd portrait poster, vertical 2:3, on a pure white
ground (kept white, not cream, not ivory): roughly eighty to a hundred clearly readable
characters — humans of every age and body type, true animals (birds, cats, dogs) and a smaller
number of humanoid characters — packed together as one interlocking "hundred faces" community
picture, not a scene with a background, not a lineup, not a grid.

All characters share the exact same hand-drawn language: loose, slightly trembling deep
grey-black ink contour lines with subtle weight variation and gentle asymmetry, filled with flat
matte opaque watercolor or marker color, features reduced to the essential (eyes as simple dots
or dashes, noses and mouths barely drawn), details kept restrained. Every human, animal and
humanoid sits at the same low level of simplification; the charm — blank, curious, sleepy,
deadpan or sly — is carried by posture and the tilt of the head rather than by facial detail.
No anime fine rendering, no pretty idealized faces, no realistic lighting or shading, no 3D,
no smooth vector curves, no airbrush, no heavy texture, no complex patterned costumes.

Composition: the sheet is packed edge to edge with mostly small and medium figures, with a
handful of larger ones interleaved for rhythm; bodies interlock like differently shaped pieces
of a hand-drawn jigsaw puzzle, each yielding space to its neighbours — round-plump next to
long-thin, lying next to standing, spread-open next to curled-up, front view next to profile
next to back view. The figures fill the sheet so completely that the white ground survives only
as thin winding seams and small irregular pockets running between the bodies, never as a wide
outer halo; the outer margin stays narrow and organic, with only a few characters grazing the
edges, never cleanly cut off. No grid, no rows, no parade line, no shared baseline, no
equidistant spacing, no single central giant protagonist, no large empty blocks. Light
overlapping is allowed when it helps the interlock, but every face and every identifying feature
stays visible; limbs never merge into one another and bodies are never wrongly connected.

Variation comes first from silhouette, proportion, pose and direction, and only second from
color. Humans vary by age (babies, children, teenagers, adults, the elderly), by body shape
(round, wiry, tall, stooped, pot-bellied), by face shape, hairstyle and temperament; most humans
are caught sitting, squatting, crouching, kneeling, lying, bending forward or stretching up —
only a few are simply standing; include a modest number of handsome adult men and attractive
adult women whose different face shapes and hairstyles make them read as distinct people rather
than one pretty template repeated. Animals keep their true species structure — a cat curled into
a closed loop, a dachshund stretched long and low on tiny legs, a bird perched or preening with
folded wings, a heavy hound with drooping ears, a hovering hummingbird, a puppy mid-pounce —
never standing on two legs, never wearing clothes. Humanoid characters each follow their own
independent logic of form — a figure built from branch and leaf, a round blunt-shelled robot
with a single lens eye, a long-necked figure with an animal head on a human body — each with its
own head-to-body proportion, never a human with a few swapped features. The humans, humanoids
and animals are distributed across the whole sheet in small interacting clusters — two children
pointing at a bird, an elderly person sharing a seat with a cat, a small robot playing with a
dog — so relationships grow out of these little groups instead of one hero surrounded by an
anonymous crowd. No two neighbouring characters share the same base silhouette, pose and
coloring; never reuse one template recolored, mirrored or rotated, never give everyone the same
big head, big eyes and identical expression; copied faces are forbidden.

Color stays natural and quiet: a shared low-to-mid-saturation base of cream white, grey-brown,
ochre brown, misty blue, blue-grey and soft green, applied as real skin, fur, feather and fabric
tones, with small accents of mustard yellow, coral orange, brick red and a clear bright blue
placed sparingly here and there. Darker greys and near-blacks are scattered as separate anchoring
notes across the sheet rather than pooled into one dark mass, and light figures are spaced
through the picture to let it breathe. The whole must never turn uniformly grey or wash out, nor
blaze with saturated color everywhere.

Rather than cutting characters for tidiness, keep every single one readable: when the sheet gets
too busy, reduce each figure's internal detail first. Seen from a distance the sheet reads as one
calm, harmonious whole; up close every single character is different from every other — dense
without blurring, full without choking, the breathing space living in the seams.

No text, no letters, no labels, no captions, no numbers, no watermark, no signature, no frame,
no border, no sticker outline, no die-cut white edge, no drop shadow, no glow, no scenery, no
ground, no sky, no background environment, no flowers, no stars, no hearts, no decorative filler
of any kind, no heavy props, no garments beyond the simplest plain clothing.
```
